Leadership Platform

What Leadership Means to Me

For me, leadership is less a position than a way of serving. Robert Greenleaf described the servant leader as someone who is “servant first,” whose wish to lead grows out of a wish to serve. My most meaningful leadership has rarely come with a title. It has usually started with noticing a need and stepping toward it.

I try to measure my leadership less by what I accomplish than by how others grow: Do the people being served grow healthier, wiser, and more able to serve others themselves?

What I Believe Leaders Should Do

Listen first. In coaching, the coachee sets the agenda, and the coach mostly asks and listens. I have found that the same approach builds trust in almost any setting, from a team huddle to a committee meeting.

Put people first. Programs matter, but people matter more. Leaders should know the goals and burdens of those they work with and help carry those burdens when they grow heavy. The BCRP Coaching Program grew from this idea: every resident deserves someone focused on their growth.

Mentoring is an investment. Some of the most important leadership happens in unscheduled moments: a question after rounds, a career conversation over coffee, a word of encouragement at the right time. These investments take time, but they compound.

Make learning safe. People grow when mistakes are treated as part of learning. Leaders set that tone by naming their own learning, asking for feedback, and offering grace when others fall short.

Communicate clearly and kindly. Honest communication is a form of respect. That means sharing the reasons behind decisions and offering growth-oriented feedback in a way that preserves dignity.

The Leader I Hope to Be

I hope to come alongside learners rather than lead from an elevated status. The best leaders I have known could sense when to step back and let others shine and when to quietly offer guidance. That balance is something I aspire to achieve.

I hope to be faithful in small things: the short debrief after a hard shift, the check-in with a struggling intern, the extra minute to make sure a family feels heard. Those moments are where trust is built.

Much of what we plant in education grows long after learners leave us, in patients we may never meet. I hope to be content with planting and watering, trusting that the growth will come.

And I hope to lead with joy, helping to lighten the load for the people around me and reminding them, and myself, why this work is worth doing.
